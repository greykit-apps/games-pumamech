# Product

## Platform

web

## Users

Desktop players of all kinds, new players first: people who never played *The Crescent Hawk's Inception* should find PumaMech inviting, and fans of the 1988 original should recognise its shape. They play with a keyboard (or a gamepad, or a mouse in fights) on a computer, in sessions of an evening; phone play is out of scope.

## Product Purpose

A remake of the 1988 mech role-playing game where the cadet is a puma: academy training and sorties, an invasion, an occupied world to survive in, a lance of mixed-species misfits to gather, round-based fights on foot and in mechs with hit locations, heat and salvage, a stock exchange, and a sealed vault opened by colour-coded keys. Success is a player who finishes the story and keeps walking Pardalis afterwards.

## Positioning

The 1988 game's full shape, rebuilt as its own game rather than an emulation. BattleTech is a live property, so every name, mech, weapon and word is ours.

## Operating Context

Part of the greykit.games portfolio, served at greykit.com/games/pumamech. It opens on the shared greykit boot screen; everything after the boot is the game's own and looks like Airborne Puma (owner's decision, 2026-10-15). Saves live in this browser only.

## Capabilities and Constraints

- Plain scripts, no build step, nothing fetched at play time.
- Desktop only; small screens get a note instead of the game.
- Keyboard first; gamepad supported; the mouse works in fights.
- The greykit boot screen (canon) stays; games-meta STANDARD.md rules still apply (credit, Forget everything, menu keys, Settings order, sound).

## Brand Commitments

- The name PumaMech; the subtitle *The Sunclaws Rise*.
- The cadet is a puma; the recruits are of many peoples (raccoon, bear, fox, otter, hare); the invaders are wolves (owner's decision, 2026-10-15).
- No BattleTech names, mechs or text.
- It must not look like a mobile game, and must not be grim: there is wit in a puma with a training mech.

## Product Principles

1. The 1988 game's feel: rounds of orders, a world that finds you, money from salvage and the market.
2. Faithful in shape, free in look and words.
3. Inviting: the journal always says what to do next.
